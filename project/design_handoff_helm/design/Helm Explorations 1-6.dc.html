4Incoming, Unsorted, Mail groups, Priorities → Topic
Five views, one app
The tabs go Incoming (triage) · Priorities · Topic (what a card opens) · Unsorted · Mail groups. The date and clock are gone; the calendar column carries a live countdown to the next meeting, counted in minutes. On-screen helper text is removed.
Reclassify is feedback
Reclassify sends the item to Unsorted and tells the model which signal made the wrong link (a thread, a sender, a keyword). It then suggests again with that correction applied. You pick the topic manually, or accept the new suggestion.
Mail write-back, never automatic
Mail groups is where bulk mail gets explored. Every action you take writes back to Thunderbird: move, mark read, delete to Trash, or create a filter. Each shows its exact count first and offers undo. The assistant can propose an action from plain language, but it only previews; you run it. Nothing is ever deleted by the app on its own.
4aIncoming. New items grouped by topic, then by mail group, with Unsorted listed separately. One reclassified row shows what happens after you press Reclassify. The calendar highlights this topic's meetings and counts down to the next one.

3Topics as the root, plus an urgency × importance lens
The graph
A topic is the root. Meetings, mail, Slack summaries, tickets, tasks, and notes hang off it. So do people, who have their own notes and link to meetings and topics too. Selecting a topic lights up its meetings on the day column.
Triage by topic
New items land in the topic the app thinks they belong to. Open the topic to see what's new, then Confirm or Reclassify each one. Reclassify moves the item to Unsorted without asking anything, and the topic keeps its context. Bulk and FYI mail never enter the list.
Urgency ≠ importance
Scoring works like TaskWarrior. Urgency comes from due dates, how long something has waited on you, meetings coming up, and who's asking. Importance comes from your category, a topic flag, and manual bumps. Every score shows its reasons, so you can disagree with it.

1Four concepts: one desktop window, dashboard + collapsible assistant pane
Brief, as I understood it
You're a manager moving into a director role. Your day is mostly meetings, plus decisions, escalations, and things you're waiting on from others, with personal life mixed in. This is a KDE window, keyboard-first and dense. The dashboard is primary; chat sits beside it and only suggests. Slack is held and delivered in the gaps between meetings. Time to the next meeting is always visible, and the day's plan is logged against what actually happened.
How the assistant gets context
No pinning. The app keeps a local index of every source that refreshes on each sync. You reference things explicitly with @ (@Priya, @PLAT-1187, @11:00). Otherwise it retrieves on its own and cites what it used, each with a sync time. Stale sources are flagged in the answer itself. Longer outputs (briefs, reviews) are written to Obsidian as notes.
Color is a first-class system
There are two layers of color. Sources get a tinted code chip. Your categories (synced from Marvin categories and Thunderbird tags, same hue everywhere) get a dot or bar. All colors share one lightness and chroma, so they read as a set against Nocturne's quiet ground.
